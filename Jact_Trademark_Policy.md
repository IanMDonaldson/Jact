# Jact Trademark Policy

Nov 5, 2026 · @Ian Donaldson

## Purpose

Most uses of the Jact name and logo need no permission at all. Jact is free, open source software, and we want people to use it, teach it, build on it, sell services around it, and redistribute it with as little friction as possible.

We protect the Jact trademarks for one reason: so that anyone who sees the name can trust that the thing they're getting really is Jact, or is clearly labeled when it isn't. This policy explains what you can do freely, the few things that need permission, and how we enforce it.

This policy covers names and logos only. The Jact source code is licensed under MIT and Apache 2.0, and nothing here limits the rights those licenses give you. The trademarks are simply a separate thing from the code.

## Uses that need no permission

You may do all of the following without asking us, including for commercial purposes, as long as you don't suggest that we made, sponsor, or endorse your work.

- **Talk about Jact.** Say your software is "written in Jact," that you're an "Jact developer," or that a job requires Jact experience. Write articles, posts, and reviews, including critical ones.
- **State compatibility.** Describe your product as "for Jact," "compatible with Jact 2.x," or "supports Jact," as long as it's true.
- **Name libraries, packages, and tools.** Use "Jact" in the name of software that works with or is built for Jact, such as "xyzt-json," "Jact Formatter," or "Jact for VS Code." Open source and commercial tools are both fine.
- **Teach Jact.** Publish books, courses, tutorials, videos, and certifications of your own with titles like "Learning Jact" or "Jact for Data Engineers," and charge for them if you like.
- **Build community.** Name user groups, meetups, forums, chat servers, podcasts, and conferences after Jact, such as "Jact Berlin" or "Jact Summit Asia."
- **Offer services.** Advertise Jact consulting, training, hosting, support, or development services.
- **Redistribute official Jact.** Ship unmodified official Jact under the name Jact, whether on its own, bundled in a product, in an operating system package, in a container image, or as part of a hosted or cloud service.

When using the Marks this way, use them to describe Jact itself, and keep your own name or brand more prominent than "Jact" in your product's branding.

## Modified versions and forks

Patched builds of Jact can keep the name; versions that change the language itself need a new one.

**You may keep the name Jact** for a modified build when your changes are limited to things like security fixes, bug fixes, backported patches, packaging and build changes, platform ports, and integration with an operating system or product. This covers Linux distributions, package managers, cloud providers, and companies that maintain their own patched builds. We ask that you indicate the build is modified, for example in the version string ("Jact 1.4.2, Acme build").

**Internal use is unrestricted.** Anything you build and use only within your own organization can be called whatever you like.

**Use a different name** for a fork or derivative that changes the language's syntax or semantics, adds or removes language features, or is otherwise not compatible with official Jact. A practical test: if code that runs correctly on official Jact would behave differently on your version, or your version fails the official Jact test suite, it needs its own name. You are welcome to describe it accurately as "based on Jact" or "a fork of Jact."

## Uses that need permission, and uses that aren't allowed

A few uses need a quick request first, and we expect to approve most of them.

- Using "Jact" in the name of a company or organization.
- Naming a commercial product or service in a way that could be mistaken for an official Jact offering, such as "Jact Enterprise," "Jact Cloud," or "Jact Studio." Descriptive names like "Acme Cloud for Jact" don't need permission.
- Registering a trademark that contains "Jact" or a confusingly similar name.
- Using a modified version of an Jact logo.
- Selling merchandise that carries the Marks (see the next section).

The following are not allowed:

- Claiming or implying that your product, service, event, or organization is official, certified, or endorsed by Jact when it isn't.
- Calling software "Jact" when it is incompatible with official Jact, as described above.
- Using the Marks in a misleading or deceptive way, including in malware, phishing, or impersonation of the Jact project or its maintainers.
- Registering domain names or social media accounts with the intent of being mistaken for the official Jact project.

## Logos, domains, merchandise, and notices

**Logos.** You may use the official, unmodified Jact logos to refer to Jact or link to the project: on slides, websites, documentation, community pages, book covers about Jact, and "Built with Jact" badges. Resizing and single-color versions are fine. Please don't otherwise alter the logos, combine them with your own logo, or make them the most prominent part of your own product's branding. Official logo files are available at \[link to official logo files\].

**Domain names.** You may use "Jact" in domain names for community sites, blogs, projects, and services, such as xyzt-tips.dev or xyztberlin.org, as long as the site doesn't pretend to be the official Jact website.

**Merchandise.** You may make stickers, shirts, and other items with the Marks for personal use or to give away at community events. If you want to sell merchandise, ask us first. We usually say yes.

**Trademark notice.** Where practical, such as in product documentation or an "About" page, please include a line like "Jact is a trademark of \[Owner name\]." This isn't needed in talks, social media posts, casual writing, or code.

## Enforcement, permission requests, and changes

**How we enforce.** We assume good faith. If we think a use doesn't fit this policy, our first step will be a friendly message explaining the concern and suggesting a fix. Legal action is a last resort, reserved for uses that are deliberately deceptive or that continue after we've tried to resolve things informally.

**Asking for permission.** Email \[trademarks@your-domain\] with a short description of what you'd like to do. We aim to reply within 14 days, and permission is free in almost all cases. If you're unsure whether you need permission, ask; we'd rather answer a question than chase a problem.

**Changes to this policy.** We may update this policy over time and will announce changes publicly. A use that this policy allowed when you began it stays allowed after a change, unless it becomes misleading.

This policy draws on the approach of the Python Software Foundation Trademark Usage Policy.
